1. Foundations

Resource Level Why it matters here
Introduction to Information Retrieval (Manning, Raghavan, Schütze), free online; chapters 1, 6 and 8 B The textbook for inverted indexes, TF-IDF, the vector space model and, in chapter 8, how retrieval is evaluated. Everything in step 4 builds on chapter 8.
Speech and Language Processing, 3rd ed. (Jurafsky, Martin), free online; chapters on embeddings, transformers and information retrieval/RAG B A clear, current introduction to word and sentence embeddings, transformers and retrieval-augmented question answering.
Google Machine Learning Crash Course B Train/validation/test splits, overfitting, classification metrics and data preparation, with exercises. Good if the general ML evaluation vocabulary is new.
scikit-learn user guide: cross-validation and model evaluation B The practical reference for splits, cross-validation and metrics, with pitfalls spelled out.
Model Evaluation, Model Selection, and Algorithm Selection in Machine Learning (Raschka, 2018) I One long tutorial covering holdout, bootstrap, cross-validation, nested cross-validation and statistical tests to compare models. The best single bridge from basics to the validation papers in section 4.
Stanford CS276: Information Retrieval and Web Search I Course slides that follow the IR book and add neural retrieval.

2. Datasets and benchmarks

How a benchmark is built decides what a score means. Read the dataset papers for the datasets you report on, not only the leaderboard.

Resource Level Why it matters here
BEIR: A Heterogeneous Benchmark for Zero-shot Evaluation of IR Models (Thakur et al., 2021) B The benchmark this project evaluates on. Explains corpus/queries/qrels, why nDCG@10 is the headline metric, and why BM25 is a strong zero-shot baseline that many dense models fail to beat.
MTEB: Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (Muennighoff et al., 2022) and the MTEB leaderboard B Where published numbers for embedding models such as all-MiniLM-L6-v2 come from, and the result format this project writes.
Fact or Fiction: Verifying Scientific Claims (Wadden et al., 2020), the SciFact paper I What a SciFact "query" really is (a scientific claim, not a question) and how relevance was judged. Explains why scores there are high.
NFCorpus: A Full-Text Learning to Rank Dataset for Medical IR (Boteva et al., ECIR 2016) I Many relevant documents per query with graded relevance, which is why Recall@k and nDCG behave differently there than on SciFact.
FiQA 2018 challenge (overview paper) I Opinion-based financial QA: informal language and long answers, a hard domain shift for general embeddings.
MS MARCO: A Human Generated MAchine Reading COmprehension Dataset (Nguyen et al., 2016) I The dataset most retrievers and cross-encoders (including ms-marco MiniLM rerankers) are trained on. Knowing it explains their strengths and blind spots.
Datasheets for Datasets (Gebru et al., 2018) B A checklist of questions to ask about any dataset: motivation, collection, labelling, intended uses. Useful for documenting your own evaluation sets.
Leakage and the Reproducibility Crisis in ML-based Science (Kapoor, Narayanan, 2022) and its companion site I A taxonomy of data leakage (train/test contamination, temporal leakage, duplicates) found across hundreds of papers. Directly relevant to tuning chunking on the same queries you report on.
"Everyone wants to do the model work, not the data work" (Sambasivan et al., CHI 2021) B Why data quality problems compound downstream ("data cascades").
Data Validation for Machine Learning (Breck et al., MLSys 2019) A How Google validates data schemas and distributions before training or serving; the idea behind tools such as TensorFlow Data Validation and Great Expectations.

3. Retrieval, embeddings and reranking

Resource Level Why it matters here
Sentence-BERT (Reimers, Gurevych, 2019) and the Sentence Transformers documentation B How bi-encoders turn sentences into vectors compared by cosine similarity, and the difference from cross-encoders. The docs explain the retrieve-then-rerank pattern this project uses.
MiniLM: Deep Self-Attention Distillation (Wang et al., 2020) A The distillation method behind all-MiniLM-L6-v2: why a 6-layer model is fast and still competitive.
The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond (Robertson, Zaragoza, 2009) I The theory behind BM25, the lexical baseline every dense retriever should be compared with, and the first half of the hybrid search on the roadmap.
Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain QA (Karpukhin et al., 2020) I The paper that made dense retrieval mainstream; introduces in-batch negatives.
Passage Re-ranking with BERT (Nogueira, Cho, 2019) I The original cross-encoder reranker (monoBERT). Explains why reranking the top-k from a fast retriever gives large nDCG gains.
Pretrained Transformers for Text Ranking: BERT and Beyond (Lin, Nogueira, Yates, 2021) A A book-length survey of neural ranking: cross-encoders, dense retrieval, multi-stage pipelines, and how to evaluate them. The reference for this whole topic.
ColBERT: Efficient and Effective Passage Search via Contextualized Late Interaction (Khattab, Zaharia, 2020) A The middle ground between bi-encoders and cross-encoders.
C-Pack: Packaged Resources to Advance General Chinese Embedding (Xiao et al., 2023), the BGE paper; Text Embeddings by Weakly-Supervised Contrastive Pre-training (E5); Nomic Embed A How current embedding models (bge-reranker, nomic-embed-text, used here) are trained with contrastive learning on large curated pairs.
Is ChatGPT Good at Search? LLMs as Re-Ranking Agents (Sun et al., 2023), RankGPT A The basis for LLM-based reranking, the second reranker in step 4.
Reciprocal Rank Fusion (Cormack, Clarke, Büttcher, SIGIR 2009) I A two-page paper with the standard way to merge BM25 and vector results. Needed for hybrid search.
Precise Zero-Shot Dense Retrieval without Relevance Labels (Gao et al., 2022), HyDE A Query rewriting by generating a hypothetical answer first; on the roadmap under query rewriting.
Evaluating Chunking Strategies for Retrieval (Chroma research, 2024) and Contextual Retrieval (Anthropic, 2024) I Two careful, reproducible looks at chunk size, overlap and adding context to chunks, the same questions as the chunking grid in step 4.

4. Validation methods and statistics

A metric without uncertainty is a guess. These explain how to decide whether configuration A is really better than B.

Resource Level Why it matters here
Cumulated Gain-based Evaluation of IR Techniques (Järvelin, Kekäläinen, 2002) I The original definition of nDCG. Worth reading once to know exactly what nDCG@10 rewards.
Pytrec_eval (Van Gysel, de Rijke, SIGIR 2018) and ir-measures (MacAvaney et al., ECIR 2022) B The scoring libraries the harness uses, and why using standard scoring code makes results comparable with published numbers.
A Comparison of Statistical Significance Tests for IR Evaluation (Smucker, Allan, Carterette, CIKM 2007) I Compares the t-test, randomization (permutation) test, bootstrap, Wilcoxon and sign tests on TREC runs. Recommends the randomization test, the kind of test behind rag-eval compare.
The Hitchhiker's Guide to Testing Statistical Significance in NLP (Dror et al., ACL 2018) I A decision tree for which test to use, and how to handle comparisons across several datasets.
With Little Power Comes Great Responsibility (Card et al., EMNLP 2020) A Statistical power: why a few hundred queries (SciFact has 300) often cannot detect a 1-point nDCG difference.
Adding Error Bars to Evals (Miller, 2024) I Confidence intervals, clustered standard errors and paired comparisons for LLM evaluations; directly applicable to RAGAS scores in step 5.
On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias (Cawley, Talbot, JMLR 2010) and Bias in Error Estimation when Using Cross-Validation for Model Selection (Varma, Simon, 2006) A Why the best score in a grid search is optimistically biased, and why you need a held-out set (or nested cross-validation) before promoting a configuration.
Accounting for Variance in Machine Learning Benchmarks (Bouthillier et al., MLSys 2021) A Sources of variance (seeds, data splits, hyperparameter search) and how many runs you need to trust a comparison.
Troubling Trends in Machine Learning Scholarship (Lipton, Steinhardt, 2018) B Short and readable: how not to over-claim from results. Good habits for writing up experiments.

5. RAG and LLM evaluation

Resource Level Why it matters here
Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks (Lewis et al., 2020) B The paper that named RAG.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Large Language Models: A Survey (Gao et al., 2023) I A map of naive, advanced and modular RAG, and of their evaluation. Useful for placing each roadmap item.
DeepLearning.AI: Building and Evaluating Advanced RAG B A two-hour hands-on course on the "RAG triad" (context relevance, groundedness, answer relevance).
RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation (Es et al., 2023) and the Ragas documentation on metrics I The framework planned for step 5: faithfulness, answer relevancy, context precision and recall, and how each is computed by an LLM judge.
ARES: An Automated Evaluation Framework for RAG Systems (Saad-Falcon et al., NAACL 2024) A Combines an LLM judge with a small human-labelled set and prediction-powered inference to give confidence intervals. The rigorous counterpart to RAGAS.
Evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Survey (Yu et al., 2024) A Compares RAG benchmarks and metrics, retrieval-side and generation-side.
Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al., 2023) and G-Eval (Liu et al., 2023) I The biases of LLM judges (position, verbosity, self-preference) and how to reduce them. Needed before trusting a local Ollama judge.
Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts (Liu et al., 2023) I Why the order and number of retrieved chunks change answer quality, which affects top-k and reranking choices.
Holistic Evaluation of Language Models (HELM) (Liang et al., 2022) A Evaluating along many axes at once (accuracy, calibration, robustness, fairness, efficiency). A model for designing your own evaluation suite.
Your AI Product Needs Evals (Husain) and Patterns for Building LLM-based Systems (Yan) B Practitioner essays on building evaluation into the development loop: unit tests for LLMs, human review, and turning failures into test cases.

6. Experiment tracking, MLOps and workflow

Resource Level Why it matters here
Rules of Machine Learning (Zinkevich, Google) B 43 practical rules from production ML: start simple, measure first, keep the pipeline testable.
MLflow documentation: tracking and model registry B The tool this project mirrors runs to: parameters, metrics, artifacts, and promoting a model through stages.
Made With ML (Mohamed) B A free course from experiment to production: data validation, testing ML code, tracking, CI/CD. Very close to this project's workflow.
Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems (Sculley et al., NeurIPS 2015) I The classic paper on why ML code is a small part of an ML system, and on entanglement, configuration debt and feedback loops.
The ML Test Score (Breck et al., 2017) I 28 concrete tests across data, model, infrastructure and monitoring, with a scoring rubric. A good checklist for the promotion quality gate.
Continuous Delivery for Machine Learning (CD4ML) (Sato, Wider, Windheuser) I Versioning data, models and code together and promoting through a pipeline, the idea behind promoting a winning run's config as a file.
MLOps: Continuous delivery and automation pipelines in ML (Google Cloud) I MLOps maturity levels 0 to 2. Useful to place the project and decide what to automate next.
Machine Learning Operations (MLOps): Overview, Definition, and Architecture (Kreuzberger et al., 2022) I A literature- and interview-based definition of MLOps principles, roles and components.
Towards CRISP-ML(Q) (Studer et al., 2020) I A process model for ML projects with quality assurance at each phase; a structured alternative to ad-hoc workflows.
Improving Reproducibility in Machine Learning Research (Pineau et al., 2020), with the NeurIPS reproducibility checklist I What must be recorded for a result to be reproducible: the reason this project stores a full config snapshot with every run.
Designing Machine Learning Systems (Huyen, O'Reilly, 2022) I A book covering data, features, evaluation, deployment and monitoring in production, end to end.
Failing Loudly: Detecting Dataset Shift (Rabanser et al., 2019) A How to detect when production queries drift away from what was evaluated.

7. Responsible AI and regulation

Resource Level Why it matters here
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the AI Act I The legal text behind eu-ai-act.md. Read Article 50 (transparency for AI systems interacting with people and generating content) and Annex III (high-risk uses) first.
NIST AI Risk Management Framework and its Generative AI Profile, NIST AI 600-1 I A practical, non-legal framework (govern, map, measure, manage) with a list of generative-AI-specific risks such as confabulation and information integrity.
OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications B Prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, excessive agency: the security risks to test for, including the open prompt-injection item.
Model Cards for Model Reporting (Mitchell et al., 2019) B A standard format for documenting a model's intended use, evaluation data and limits; complements the model inventory.
ISO/IEC 42001: AI management systems A The management-system standard organisations adopt to show AI governance; useful context when the project meets enterprise compliance.
